What the research says

DeutschFrançaisEspañol

Myofunctional therapy is a set of exercises for the tongue, soft palate, cheeks and throat. The idea is simple: muscles that keep their tone during sleep are less likely to let the airway narrow and vibrate. These are the studies the method rests on.

A randomized trial in sleep apnea, 2009

31 adults with moderate sleep apnea did either 3 months of daily mouth and throat exercises or a sham therapy. The exercise group snored less, was less sleepy in the day and had less severe sleep apnea on a sleep study. The sham group did not change.

Source: Guimarães et al., Am J Respir Crit Care Med, 2009

A randomized trial in snoring, 2015

39 adults with snoring or mild to moderate sleep apnea did either 3 months of daily mouth exercises or a control programme of breathing exercises. Snoring was measured with recordings rather than asked about. It fell in the exercise group and did not change in the control group.

Source: Ieto et al., Chest, 2015 (NCT01636856)

A review of the evidence, 2015

A meta-analysis pooled the adult studies available at the time, nine studies with 120 people. Sleep apnea severity, snoring and daytime sleepiness all fell after therapy. The authors suggest the exercises as an addition to other treatments rather than a replacement.

Source: Camacho et al., Sleep, 2015

What the studies do not tell you

  • They are small, and most ran for about three months.
  • People practised daily with a therapist checking in. Doing it alone, every day, is the hard part.
  • They tested the exercise method, not Snore Fit or any other app.
  • Results differ from person to person.
  • The exercises are not a treatment for moderate or severe sleep apnea. If you use CPAP or an oral appliance, keep using it.

How Snore Fit uses this

The program follows the trials: daily practice over 12 weeks, the same muscle groups, a demonstration of every exercise. Because practising alone is the hard part, sessions are short and a weekly tongue-strength test shows progress before the snoring changes.

Snore Fit is a wellness app, not a medical device, and does not diagnose or treat sleep apnea.
