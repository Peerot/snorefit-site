How to choose a snoring exercise app

DeutschFrançaisEspañol

Mouth and throat exercises (myofunctional therapy) have been studied for snoring for more than fifteen years. You can read the exercises anywhere for free. What an app adds is whether you keep doing them for the weeks it takes. These are the questions worth asking first.

1. Is the plan built around you?

Snoring has different sources: a mouth that falls open, a tongue that slides back, a soft palate that vibrates. An app that gives everyone the same routine trains all of them equally. A better one asks a few questions first and leans the workout toward your weak spots, without dropping the rest.

2. Does it have a finish line?

The trials ran daily for 8 to 12 weeks. A routine with no end date is hard to keep up. A plan with a date you can see, followed by a lighter maintenance routine, is easier to finish.

3. How much time does it ask for?

Two sessions a day of 15 to 20 minutes ask a lot of anyone. Once a day, about five minutes, fits after brushing your teeth, and a habit that fits is a habit that lasts.

4. Can you see progress before the snoring changes?

Snoring takes weeks to change, and most people give up before it does. Look for something that changes sooner: a timed tongue-strength test, or a short morning log of how you slept and what your partner heard.

5. Does it fit around your life?

  • Can you keep your music or a podcast playing while you train?
  • Does it show what happens inside your mouth, not only the outside of a face?
  • Does it work without an account?
  • Does it ask you to do anything odd, like pressing your tongue on the phone screen?

6. How does it charge?

Prefer an app that lets you try a real session before paying, explains its free trial up front and reminds you before the trial ends. Be wary of weekly billing: nobody notices a change in their snoring within a week.

When an app is not the answer

If someone has seen you stop breathing in your sleep, you wake up choking or gasping, or you are very sleepy during the day, see a doctor. Those are signs of sleep apnea, which needs a diagnosis, not an exercise app.
